Emotion Regulation Skills

Check the Facts

Check the Facts is a DBT skill that allows you to analyze if your emotional intensity and action urges fit the facts of a given situation.

Marsha Linehan, founder of DBT, has a great resource for utilizing Check the Facts in her DBT manual. Check it out here!

Check the Facts (*my way*)

If you, like myself, have a difficult time using this skill the traditional way, see below for my modifications.

  1. Check in with yourself:
    • Are you experiencing strong emotions
    • Can you identify what they are?

  2. Reflect on the facts of the situation by...
    • Think back to the situation that prompted your strong emotions
    • Pretend you are an external observer narrating the prompting event.
      • It can help to pretend you are an author telling the story of the prompting event using a third-party perspective (stories told in the third-person have no access to the internal thoughts and feelings of the characters, they are based on external observations alone)
    • Review your external observations of the prompting event. Is it entirely based on external facts? If not, revise it.
    • Return to your first-party perspective of the prompting event. List the facts of your emotional experience. What are/were you feeling? Stick to your emotional experience only, not the behaviors/action urges.
    • Were there any vulnerability factors that may have impacted how you are/were feeling? For example, were you hungry, did you have a difficult day, etc. The Minfulness Emotional Check-In skill can help with identifying this.

  3. Complete another self check-in. Identify what you are feeling and how strong the intensity of the emotion is (see the Mindfulness Page to practice using SUDS). In this step, it is important to recognize that your emotions are valid! It is okay to feel what you are feeling, regardless of what it is. Try and observe your emotions without judgement.

  4. Identify what action urges you are experiencing. Again, try and avoid self-judgement.

  5. Now for the most important step: does your emotional intensity and/or your action urges fit the facts? Ask yourself...
    • Are there vulnerability factors impacting your emotional intensity that are external to the prompting event? How can you manage these factors to bring down your emotional intensity?
    • Are your action urges productive in improving the situation (in other words, do they fit the facts)? Action urges that involve harming yourself or others NEVER fit the facts. If your action urges do not fit the facts, brainstorm other responses that better fit the situation
    • Does your emotional intensity AND action urge(s) fit the facts?

    • If the answer is YES, move into problem solving mode. This may involve approaching the situation itself or managing the emotions you feel about the situation.
    • If the answer is NO, try using the Distress Tolerance Page for managing intense emotions, or the Cope Ahead Skill to identify helpful tools you can use to combat the problem

Cope Ahead

Use cope ahead to prepare for potentially difficult situations. The Cope Ahead skill helps you identify skills and tools you may need in the future, so you do not need to brainstorm skills when in moments of high distress
